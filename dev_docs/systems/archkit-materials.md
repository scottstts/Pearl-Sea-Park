# Archkit & materials (S6; craft pass 2026-07-13)

- **`materials/library.ts` (ParkMaterials)** — the park's whole identity, built ONCE via MaterialsSystem (init-time, after medium provides the caustic sampler). Brass, verdigris, marble, nacre, iron, glass, lamp globes (emissive avg 2.6 with a mantle mottle — inside bloom's hierarchy), worldspace mosaic, dark wood, canvas, foliage, soil, japanned lacquer, oxblood saddle leather, and hemp rope (fenders/rigging/coils). All procedural TSL, all caustic-lit. Never instantiate ad-hoc materials in districts — extend the library.
- **Material doctrine (2026-07-13 craft pass)**: every channel of a material derives from the SAME few named causal fields (brass: hammer+tarnish; marble: warp→two vein families+bed; verdigris: one patina field driving color, metalness, AND roughness; mosaic: tile id → palette, glaze roughness, and a grout-bevel normal), and fine microstructure multiplies a camera-distance `detailKeep` so it dissolves before it aliases. Nacre is a cosine-palette interference sweep whose phase also drives growth-ripple roughness. When adding a material, follow this recipe — never one independent noise per channel.
- **`detailKeep` is camera DISTANCE, and that is not good enough for a large flat surface.** At grazing incidence a pixel's footprint on a floor grows as distance² / eye height while the distance itself grows only linearly, so a distance fade still leaves dozens of tiles inside one pixel a short way down the esplanade — the same failure the ocean documents as the "horizon comb". The mosaic therefore retires every band on `cell.fwidth()`, the footprint of one output pixel measured in TILES, which is correct at any angle, resolution, or FOV and carries no authored metre thresholds. Reach for `detailKeep` only on small or non-planar set dressing; on anything floor-like use the footprint. The two are interchangeable head-on: at 6.5 tiles/m, footprint ≈ 0.0065 × distance, so `detailKeep(30)` is `smoothstep(0.09, 0.2, footprint)`.
- **Prefilter a periodic band by integrating it; do not widen it.** The mosaic's grout is a smoothstep ramp `GROUT_EDGE` wide on each side of a cell boundary. Widening that ramp with the footprint is the obvious move and it is wrong: a wider ramp removes more coverage, so distant paving would slide toward the grout colour as it smoothed. Instead note that a smoothstep ramp averages 0.5, so the authored band costs exactly `GROUT_EDGE` of coverage per cell — identical to a HARD line of that width straddling the boundary. That hard line has a closed-form running integral, and a finite difference of it over the footprint is the exact box average at every scale, converging to `1 − GROUT_EDGE` by itself. Measured: the per-axis mean stays at 0.91000 for footprints from 0 to 200 tiles per pixel, so the filter cannot brighten or darken the floor anywhere along its length — no seam to hunt for. Contrast collapses smoothly and is flat by one tile per pixel. **Match the means of the crisp and filtered branches before you cross-fade them; that is the whole trick.**
- **Fade per-cell values to the field's MEASURED mean, not to the value at the mean input.** The mosaic's `id` and `glaze` are constant across a cell, so they carry no information at all once a pixel spans one; past that they are per-pixel noise, which is what makes tesserae crawl. `MOSAIC_MEAN_TESSERA` is the colour integrated over the park's whole tile range against the real `valueNoise2` field. Feeding the palette the average `id` instead gives (0.573, 0.718, 0.703) against the true (0.668, 0.742, 0.695) — the palette is a pair of smoothsteps in `id`, so it is nonlinear and `E[f(x)] ≠ f(E[x])`. That 0.1 error in red is a visible hue-and-brightness seam exactly where the fade completes. Recompute the constant if the palette moves.
- **Mosaic bevel normals follow the same space contract as sand ripples.** `MeshStandardNodeMaterial.normalNode` consumes a view-space normal. Author the mosaic's grout-bevel perturbation in its geometry-local/world-aligned floor space, normalize it there, and pass it through `transformNormalToView()` exactly once. Returning that vector directly makes its sun response depend on camera yaw and pitch.
- **`archkit/writer.ts` (SlotWriter)** — modules emit cloned+transformed geometry per material slot; `compile()` merges to one Mesh per material (procedural-architecture skill). An entire district = ~10 draw calls.
- **`archkit/modules.ts` (ArchKit)** — the civic vocabulary includes layered/dentilled cornices, finished two-rail balustrades (profiled rail + brass half-round cap, vase balusters), brass-inlaid curbed paths, furnished café tables, and pearl-topped gate urns in addition to columns, arches, domes, roofs, plazas, and ticket machines. Columns are genuinely fluted solids (18 concave flutes + classical entasis, `flutedShaftGeometry`) under an ogee capital with a verdigris leaf ring; arches carry an inner brass moulding rib, a marble keystone, and springing rosettes; domes add latitude glazing rings and a nacre tip pearl; gable roofs get brass glazing bars and ridge-end finials; plazas get a compass inlay ring and a raised nacre centre medallion (never a coplanar disc); the ticket machine is a full instrument (nacre dial, bezel, hands, side crank, slot lip, claw feet). Geometry prototypes are cached per kit instance; district output is compiled by material slot. Real meters everywhere.
- **`world/parkAmenities.ts` (ParkAmenitiesSystem)** — benches and lamps are immutable local-space prototypes placed only through `InstancedMesh` transforms. A bench uses three shared material meshes; a lamp uses four. Individual components can no longer cross `SlotWriter` chunks or receive different placement transforms. Lamp arms are point-to-point members whose first endpoint is the pole junction and whose last endpoint penetrates the globe socket. Benches are placed by an explicit facing target, never a raw guessed yaw. `npm run audit:geometry` checks finite vertices, bounds, bench joinery/orientation, arm contact, and the notice-board roof endpoints.
- **`world/facilitySigns.ts` (FacilitySignsSystem)** — one rooted Belle Époque marker prototype identifies every guest-facing facility (17, including the `park-entrance` teleport node beside the Descent Bell landing). Three instanced frame slots plus one merged atlas face keep the full wayfinding set to four draws; the atlas grid is count-derived (`ceil(n/4)` rows). Sign placement and approach targets live in `world/parkLayout.ts`; the signs use the terrain height authority and receive matching static colliders, and each doubles as a teleport node (`player/teleport.ts`).
- **Rotation lesson**: torus prototypes already live in a VERTICAL (XY) plane — arches and dome ribs need only scale+yaw. Composing "fix-up" rotations flattened the ribs invisibly; check silhouettes from inside AND outside.
- Districts receive `DistrictServices { physics, materials, amenities, seats?, interaction? }` — seats/interaction are absent in `?view=` inspection mode, so register conditionally.
- Glass is now a physical dielectric rather than constant-alpha color:
  transmission 1, IOR 1.52, 5 cm optical thickness, roughness 0.035,
  clearcoat, subtle cyan Beer-Lambert attenuation, and the shared PMREM sky.
  Three r185's physical node material captures the opaque viewport in-place,
  so refraction needs no extra scene render. Keep alpha blending disabled and
  opacity 1; transmission itself puts the material in the transparent render
  list and performs the backdrop composition. Glass remains `depthWrite=false`
  and writes zero to the normal MRT's AO-receiver alpha: pairing a near glass
  normal with distant opaque depth makes GTAO stripe curved shells. `SlotWriter`
  must treat both alpha-blended and transmissive materials as non-opaque so
  glass roofs and domes never cast plywood shadows.
- `world/parkLayout.ts` owns static anchors, path segments, and facility
  entrances; `world/parkPlan.ts` derives footprint fields from them.
- Atrium (`world/districts/atrium.ts`) is the hero reference: colonnade ring with entrance gaps, arches, dome, benches with real seats, lamps with PointLights (intensity ~6.5, distance 13 — four of them; keep local light counts modest), plaza collider (`addStaticCylinder`), ticket machine emitting `ticket/punched`.
- `world/parkFacilities.ts` is the finish-plan authority for civic sites. It keeps Esplanade, Tidal Court, Midway, Café, Observatory, Overlook, and Atrium compositions distinct while emitting through the same `SlotWriter`; do not fold these plans back into `parkAssembly.ts` or replace them with unstructured prop scatter.
